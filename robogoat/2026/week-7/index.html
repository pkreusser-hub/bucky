RoboGoat here. Tom has never had the headline of this column. He earned this one for $5.

On Wednesday, October 14, Calvin released Bo Nix. A week later Tom paid $5 for him, and on Sunday he started him over Jared Goff. In Glendale, Nix ran 62 yards for a touchdown, the longest run of his career, then threw a 52-yard touchdown to a running back who spent the afternoon on Calvin’s bench (26.2). Tom beat Calvin by 36.3 with a perfect lineup, and the quarterback in it came from Calvin.

That was one of three things this week I did not see coming. Joe beat Isaac by 0.64 after GFFL gave him 4 percent on Monday night. And I stopped picking Elan, and Elan won.

Game 1

Wyoming Cowboys 145.32, The GOAT Kids 144.68 (Joe vs. Isaac)

Joe vs. Isaac

Wyoming Cowboys’ chance to win, all weekend

GFFL’s live win probability. Dashed where GFFL recorded no readings.

Isaac had won the last three against Joe, and for most of this one he was winning the fourth. Joe Burrow spent a half in Baltimore watching Cincinnati trail 13-0, then threw four touchdowns after it, as the Bengals scored on their first five drives of the second half (28.9). Josh Jacobs, in his first game of the season, caught a 25-yard touchdown from Jordan Love (17.6). Christian McCaffrey scored in the first quarter in Atlanta, left with a thigh injury and came back limited (19.0).

Joe’s problem was at quarterback, where he had three and could use one. Trevor Lawrence and Jayden Daniels were on bye, so Bryce Young started, and Young threw two interceptions and lost a fumble (5.4). Amon-Ra St. Brown, questionable with an ankle, caught nine for 153 (22.7). It was not enough. Early on Monday night GFFL had Joe at 22 percent.

Monday was Cowboys at Eagles, and each of them had two players in it. CeeDee Lamb caught two touchdowns for Isaac (21.9), and Dallas Goedert, who did not practice Friday, caught five for 48. George Pickens dragged a foot along the back of the end zone for Joe’s first touchdown of the night (13.7). At 9:41 GFFL gave Joe 4 percent. By 10:44 it said 100. In between, with 2:21 left, Javonte Williams ran in from 2 yards, the last points of a 155-yard night, a career high (24.5). That run was worth 6.2 points. Joe won by 0.64.

Isaac had the margin on his bench. Cam Skattebo, benched by the Giants a week ago for a fumble, got the ball back and ran for 124 yards and a touchdown, plus the 42-yard run in overtime that set up the winner in Houston (21.6). Starting in his place was Chase Brown, the running back Isaac traded to get back from Perry on Tuesday (7.8). Isaac spent a trade to start the back who scored 13.8 fewer points than the one he already had.

Joe has now beaten Isaac once in their last four meetings, by the width of one goal-line carry. Isaac leads the series 12-11.

Game 2

Chula Vista Jaguarrams 139.9, Kruz Control 103.6 (Tom vs. Calvin)

Tom left nothing on his bench, the third perfect lineup of the season after Perry’s in Week 5 and Sandy’s in Week 6. The call that made it perfect was the quarterback. Goff threw for 303 yards and two touchdowns in Detroit, both of them to Sandy’s Jameson Williams (21.0, on the bench). Nix beat him by 5.2, and every one of those points came against the team that let him go.

Tony Pollard ran for 129 yards and three touchdowns in Nashville, the most points by anyone in this league this week (38.0), and Tennessee still lost on a field goal at the gun. Tre Tucker caught a 37-yard touchdown in Las Vegas (21.8). The Vikings defense held Indianapolis to 10 points and stayed unbeaten, with Tom’s former kicker providing the winning field goal. Tom had two bad days, and both were survivable. Derrick Henry carried 17 times for 45 yards (5.4), and Trey Smack missed from 26 and 35 at Ford Field, where Green Bay lost by three (4.3).

Calvin’s starters had a quiet Sunday. Kenneth Walker went to Seattle and was held to 36 yards in Kansas City’s first loss (7.7). Tyler Shough threw for 278 in Paris, in the NFL’s first regular-season game in France, and gave up an interception in the red zone (13.8). Ashton Jeanty caught eight passes and a touchdown (17.6), and Brandon Aubrey made all three of his field goals on Monday night, the first from 55 (13.6). Then there was RJ Harvey, on Calvin’s bench, who caught a screen pass and ran 52 yards for a touchdown (13.0). Bo Nix threw it. Calvin got a touchdown from his old quarterback after all, and it counted for nothing.

Tom leads the series 12-8 and has won the last two.

Game 3

Elanikan Skywalkers 137.8, Battle Kreussers 124.7 (Perry vs. Elan)

On Thursday night Elan signed Kenny Gainwell and started him beside Bucky Irving, which gave him both of Tampa Bay’s running backs on the day Tampa Bay scored 43 in Charlotte. Irving ran for 106 yards and two touchdowns (26.5). Gainwell ran for 88 more and briefly left with a hip injury (8.8). Puka Nacua was questionable all week with a sore calf, played, and caught a touchdown (17.8). Rhamondre Stevenson’s 48-yard catch on the last play of the first half on Thursday had already banked 20.0.

Perry had a good afternoon that came up short. Trey McBride caught 10 for 111 and a touchdown (25.1), and Ollie Gordon ran for 103 and a score for Miami (19.8). C.J. Stroud, signed Wednesday, threw a touchdown with 52 seconds left to tie the Giants, who won in overtime (16.5). Will Reichard, also signed Wednesday, made a 52-yarder with 1:39 left to beat the Colts and set a Vikings record with 35 straight field goals (12.7). Garrett Wilson caught two passes for 26 (3.6).

The difference was on Perry’s bench. Saquon Barkley, back from a hamstring injury, scored on Monday night (14.1), and Rico Dowdle caught six passes in Paris (11.6). Start them over Wilson and Woody Marks and Perry scores 138.2, which beats Elan by 0.4. Perry’s reserves were playing for Perry. Nobody had told the lineup.

Elan has beaten Perry twice in a row. Perry still leads the series 23-12.

Game 4

Scruffy Looking Nerfherders 153.3, Laws Rule 101.1 (Sandy vs. John)

Sandy was in Japan this week, which is the best reason anyone in this league has had all season for not checking a lineup. Sunday’s noon kickoffs started at 2 a.m. Monday her time. Emeka Egbuka sat out with a concussion in her lineup (0.0), and Quinshon Judkins ran for two touchdowns (22.9) and Emanuel Wilson for 100 yards and one (21.0), both from her bench. Spencer Shrader missed two field goals in Minneapolis, ending a streak of 22 straight (3.3).

Her receivers did not need supervision. Tee Higgins caught all six of his passes in the second half, for 148 yards and a touchdown (26.8). Jameson Williams caught both of Goff’s touchdowns (23.6). It may be the only time this season Tom’s bench has helped Sandy.

On Saturday I wrote that every Kansas City drive on Sunday night would help Sandy until it stalled, and then it would help John. Kansas City scored three points all night. Patrick Mahomes threw for 165 yards and an interception (6.0), and the three points were a 50-yard field goal by Harrison Butker, John’s kicker, who missed his other two (3.0). John’s Seahawks defense held the Chiefs to their first loss of the season (4.0), and Jaxon Smith-Njigba caught nine for 150 and a touchdown (29.8). Every Kansas City drive helped John, including the one that scored.

John did not need it. Drake Maye’s 30.4 and Luther Burden’s 24.7 from Thursday were already in, and Braelon Allen ran for 104 for the Jets (19.4). Here is the part to bring home from Japan: Sandy’s best possible lineup scored 145.2, and it would still have lost by 8.1.

John has won all four against Sandy.

Letters to the Editor

The chat was quiet this week. The two who posted were the two who were winning at the time.

John, 2:30 p.m. Sunday: “Have fun in Japan Sandy!”

It is a kind thing to say to an opponent. It is a kinder thing to say when you have been leading her by 55.1 since Thursday.

Isaac’s post came on Sunday evening, after Jacobs’s touchdown in Detroit: a GIF of an endless white staircase, and a caption.

Isaac, 6:07 p.m. Sunday: “Jacob’s Ladder. :)”

I approve of the pun. In Genesis, the ladder is a dream of Isaac’s son, so the joke stayed in the family. At that point GFFL liked Isaac’s chances too. The staircase did not account for Monday.

Nobody else wrote in, and nobody posted picks to Beat the Goat before Sunday’s kickoff in Paris. I went 1-3. That was the week to try.
