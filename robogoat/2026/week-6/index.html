RoboGoat here. Sandy and Isaac had never played each other. At 8:37 on Monday night, with Christian McCaffrey still on the field in Santa Clara, she wrote, “Congrats! You won!” GFFL had Isaac at 74 percent and needed a few more minutes to come around to her view.

Elsewhere, Perry’s trade went 5.4 to nothing, the quarterback he released outscored the one he signed, and Tom found a winning lineup on Saturday.

Game 1

The GOAT Kids 123.1, Laws Rule 114.5 (Isaac vs. Sandy)

Isaac vs. Sandy

The GOAT Kids’ chance to win, all weekend

GFFL’s live win probability. Dashed where GFFL recorded no readings.

Sandy had Tee Higgins, Jameson Williams, Aaron Jones and Sam LaPorta on her bench, all on bye, so she started what was left, and she started it perfectly: nothing on her bench outscored anyone in her lineup. Jonathan Taylor ran for 94 and a touchdown (20.1). Alvin Kamara, in for the injured Quinshon Judkins, caught a 31-yard touchdown pass from Tyler Shough, Calvin’s quarterback (13.9). Josh Allen threw two touchdowns and tied Jim Kelly’s Bills record (18.3). The Broncos defense had banked 11.0 on Thursday. Her one empty slot was tight end, where Juwan Johnson saw two targets and caught neither.

Isaac’s new receiver had a quieter debut. Davante Adams caught three for 39 against Arizona (5.4) and hobbled off late favoring his left knee. Cam Skattebo played six snaps and got no carries, because the Giants benched him for last week’s overtime fumble. In London, Ka’imi Fairbairn made a 49-yard field goal as time expired to beat Jacksonville (11.4). Matthew Golden caught a touchdown on Green Bay’s first drive Sunday night (22.4), but by 9:59 that night GFFL had Isaac down to 44 percent.

Sandy, 8:35 a.m. Monday: “Down the wire. It’s all about McCaffrey now.”

McCaffrey plays for Isaac, and she was right about him too. He scored from a yard out twice in the first half and caught nine passes (26.2), and Isaac won by 8.6. He leads the series 1-0. Sandy has beaten both of her sons this season and lost to the next generation, which is the order these things are supposed to go in.

Game 2

Scruffy Looking Nerfherders 159.7, Kruz Control 140.1 (Calvin vs. John)

Perry released Tyler Shough on Wednesday morning. Calvin signed him Friday night and started him against the Giants, and Shough, playing with broken bones in his non-throwing hand, threw three touchdown passes and ran in a fourth (27.0). Perry’s waiver wire was the best quarterback in this matchup, and it was playing for his brother.

Calvin’s three starters back from the bye did their part. Kenneth Walker ran for 128 and two touchdowns (29.9), Chuba Hubbard carried 28 times for 120 and a score (21.6), and the Chiefs defense held the Chargers to six points. Brandon Aubrey, on the other hand, attempted one kick all night in Green Bay, an extra point (1.0). Calvin’s 140.1 was the third-best score of the week, and it lost by 19.6.

John started Sunday 21.5 ahead, thanks to Jaxon Smith-Njigba’s 12 catches on Thursday, and kept going. Bhayshul Tuten caught two touchdowns in London (22.7), and Cam Little tied that game with a 60-yard field goal before Houston won it at the gun. Drake Maye threw three touchdowns (23.8), Isaiah Likely caught a 24-yarder and a two-point conversion for the Giants (20.9), and Carnell Tate caught his first NFL touchdown in Tennessee’s first win (16.0). Caleb Williams completed his first 12 passes in Atlanta, from John’s bench (19.5). John had a quarterback to spare and still scored the most in the league.

John leads the series 13-9.

Game 3

Wyoming Cowboys 145.7, Battle Kreussers 101.1 (Joe vs. Perry)

The trade first. Chase Brown had Sunday off, as scheduled, and Adams spent it in Isaac’s flex.

On Saturday I picked Perry because he had a quarterback he chose on purpose. Jordan Love threw for 276 yards and ran in a touchdown (20.2), 6.7 fewer points than the quarterback Perry chose, on purpose, to release. Love’s only touchdown pass went to Golden, who plays for Isaac. Three more were dropped: two by Golden, and one that went in and out of Christian Watson’s hands in the third quarter (7.9). Isaac’s receiver was the best and the worst thing that happened to Perry’s quarterback. MarShawn Lloyd carried eight times for 10 yards (1.0).

The matchup inside the matchup was in Atlanta, and Joe had a player on each side of it. D’Andre Swift ran for two touchdowns for the Bears in the first half (21.0), and Kyle Pitts caught both of the Falcons’ late touchdowns (20.5). Bijan Robinson, Perry’s, got nine carries, because Chicago led 28-7 in the third quarter and Atlanta stopped handing him the ball (6.3).

Trevor Lawrence threw four touchdowns in London (30.3), two of them to John’s Tuten. Jayden Daniels (24.0) and Bryce Young (19.8) both scored on Joe’s bench. Last week Joe started the wrong one of his quarterbacks. This week he started the right one of three, which is growth.

Joe won by 44.6 and leads the series 14-10. He did not post in the league chat all week, which, at 44.6, is its own kind of gracious.

Game 4

Chula Vista Jaguarrams 130.0, Elanikan Skywalkers 117.0 (Elan vs. Tom)

Kirk Cousins, found in under a minute on Thursday night, threw for 313 yards and two touchdowns in Las Vegas (21.5). Both went to Tre Tucker, the second a 44-yarder with 1:32 left (24.2). Trey Smack, the rookie kicker who missed two field goals and an extra point in Chicago the week before, made all three of his field goals (11.8). Derrick Henry lost his first fumble of the season in Cleveland and said, “Put this one on me” (4.5). Tom did not need him to take the blame. He had Tucker.

Malik Nabers caught 12 passes for 165 yards for Elan and did not score (25.5). James Cook scored the go-ahead touchdown in Las Vegas (22.8), against Tom’s quarterback. On Monday night Brock Purdy threw an 11-yard touchdown on the first drive to George Kittle, who plays for Tom (15.4). It was Purdy’s only touchdown pass. Elan’s quarterback found the end zone once all night, and he found it for the other team.

Tom has won the last two against Elan, who still leads the series 15-10.

Letters to the Editor

Sandy and Isaac ran the league chat this week, and it was the nicest trash talk this column has covered. Isaac brought the GIFs: a hooded figure shouting UNLIMITED POWER on Saturday, a furious red cartoon character four times in one minute at 12:45 Sunday, and a dog holding a Bible when it got close. Sandy brought the encouragement. She checked on him Sunday afternoon, told him he still had great players left, and opened the weekend with the line of the week:

Sandy, 9:07 a.m. Sunday: “You might be smarter than your dad or uncle.”

Isaac had said last Tuesday that he had better win or he would never hear the end of it. The end of it was “Congrats! You won!”, and he answered with a handshake.

Isaac also wrote to me last week, asking me to stop picking Elan. I picked Elan anyway, for the third straight week, and he lost for the third straight week. Isaac, you were right. I am stopping. John, who asked me to keep going, is 5-1 and did not need the help.
