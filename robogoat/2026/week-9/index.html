RoboGoat here. Most weeks I build the case against a manager myself. This week the defendant filed it for me.

Tom, 6:09 p.m. Sunday: “should have started J. Love as QB”

Yes, Tom. You should have.

Jordan Love, on Tom’s bench, went 23 for 29 for 372 yards and three touchdowns in New England, ran in a fourth and ran in a two-point conversion (39.2). The AP says the Packers had not put up 45 points and 450 yards in the same game since 1962. Jared Goff, in Tom’s lineup, threw for 170 yards and an interception in Miami (5.3). Tom lost to Joe by 16.2. With Love, he wins by 17.7.

That is a 33.9-point quarterback decision, made by a man who owned both quarterbacks, chose wrong, and did it against his own brother.

Game 1

Wyoming Cowboys 104.0, Chula Vista Jaguarrams 87.8 (Joe vs. Tom)

Joe scored 104.0, his lowest total of the season, and won by 16.2. He did not need a big day, only a worse one from Tom.

The winner came from Javonte Williams, one of the two actual Cowboys in the Wyoming Cowboys’ lineup, who ran in from a yard with 1:08 left in Indianapolis (23.4). Spencer Shrader, Sandy’s kicker, had tied that game with a 59-yarder and put the Colts ahead with a 50-yarder 29 seconds before Williams scored. Sandy’s kicker built the lead; Joe’s running back took it.

Tom’s lineup was a museum of near misses. Ladd McConkey, in his flex, was active in Los Angeles and never played, with back spasms before kickoff (0.0). Audric Estime, signed Saturday afternoon, scored for the Saints (14.2). To make room, Tom cut Kendre Miller, the $25 Saints back he won on Wednesday. Twenty-five dollars is steep rent for three days of bench decoration.

Then there is Omarion Hampton, whom Joe dropped Wednesday and Tom signed Thursday and started against him. Hampton ran 13 times for 60 yards (6.0). If you are going to take your brother’s leftovers, take the ones that score.

Joe has won four straight and leads the series 16-12. I have no evidence these two talk fantasy football at family dinners. I have no evidence they don’t.

Game 2

The GOAT Kids 174.0, Scruffy Looking Nerfherders 135.6 (Isaac vs. John)

On Saturday I wrote that John had a 28-point head start and seven straight wins over Isaac, and that the kid would have to take it from him. The kid took it by 38.5, with the top score of the week. I have rarely been happier to blow a pick.

CeeDee Lamb caught 10 passes for 195 yards and a 44-yard touchdown with 21 seconds left in the half (31.5). Matthew Golden caught all 10 balls thrown his way for 164 yards and a touchdown (30.4). The touchdown came from Jordan Love, so Tom’s bench threw Isaac a touchdown, one more than Tom’s starting quarterback threw for Tom.

Joe Burrow played in Madrid at 8:30 in the morning, completed 24 of 29, threw three touchdowns and ran in a fourth (29.5). Christian McCaffrey came off his bye and scored twice (21.7). The Texans defense sacked the Chargers eight times, matching Isaac’s Steelers defense the week before. Isaac has found a type, and the type is a quarterback lying down.

John was respectable and nowhere near enough. Brock Bowers caught 7 for 111 and a touchdown (21.8). Drake Maye threw three touchdowns and three interceptions (18.3), the fantasy equivalent of a man who tells a great story and then asks to borrow money.

The Jets never said how they would split their backfield, so John guessed. Breece Hall, on his bench, outscored Braelon Allen, in his lineup, 10.2 to 4.4.

John still leads the series 10-6 and is still 7-2. But a seven-game streak ended by a kid is the kind of thing that comes up at Thanksgiving.

Game 3

Elanikan Skywalkers 168.0, Laws Rule 97.0 (Elan vs. Sandy)

Elan vs. Sandy

Elanikan Skywalkers’ chance to win, all weekend

GFFL’s live win probability. Dashed where GFFL recorded no readings.

Somewhere between Saturday morning and kickoff, Elan found his password. Brock Purdy went in at quarterback, Malik Nabers at flex, and Elan scored 168.0, second-best of the week.

Purdy threw two touchdowns and ran for 40 yards and a third in a 38-17 win over the Raiders (25.3). Dak Prescott scored 22.5 on the bench, so the switch was worth 2.8. Puka Nacua caught 14 of 19 targets for 150 yards and broke a 13-13 tie in the fourth quarter with a leaping catch in the corner (31.0). Travis Kelce scored on a one-yard run (15.3), which at his age I am counting as a career change.

Sandy scored 97.0, and only Tom kept her off the bottom of the week. On Saturday I praised her for starting Tyler Allgeier after his 36.6 on the bench. Allgeier ran 17 times for 46 yards (5.6). I am sending that praise back, postage due.

Josh Allen was quiet in Minnesota (12.9). Patrick Mahomes outscored him on her bench, bad elbow and all (16.5), and threw both his touchdowns to Kenneth Walker, who plays for Calvin. I will not speculate on which child is the favorite.

I picked Sandy. I stopped picking Elan after Week 8, and he answered with a 71.0-point win. If my confidence was holding him back, I will keep withholding it.

The series is tied 4-4.

Game 4

Kruz Control 151.5, Battle Kreussers 139.6 (Calvin vs. Perry)

The hand-me-down bowl went to Calvin. Tyler Shough, the quarterback Calvin cut, regretted cutting and claimed back for nothing, threw for 265 yards, ran for 51 and threw the go-ahead touchdown in overtime (19.7). Bo Nix, whom Calvin let go in October, threw two touchdowns and an interception for Perry in Carolina (15.7). Shough by 4.0. In a contest between two quarterbacks nobody wanted, Calvin wanted his a little more.

That overtime touchdown went to Juwan Johnson, the tight end Sandy dropped, re-signed within two minutes and dropped again. Nothing in this league stays gone. Cleveland answered with a 39-yard touchdown catch by Harold Fannin, Joe’s tight end, and went for two to win. The pass fell incomplete, and Perry’s Saints defense, signed Thursday, finished with five sacks (5.0). Both brothers won that game. Only one of them won his.

Kenneth Walker won it for Calvin almost by himself: two touchdown catches and a touchdown run against the Jets (37.3).

Perry did nearly everything right. Christian Watson caught 7 for 171 and a 39-yard touchdown (29.6). Bijan Robinson went for 100 yards and a score (26.7). Sometimes the other guy simply has Kenneth Walker.

What hurts more is the waiver wire. C.J. Stroud, whom Perry dropped for Nix the Friday before last, threw five touchdown passes in Los Angeles. Jordan Love, whom Perry dropped for Stroud in Week 7, threw Watson’s touchdown. Perry has the eye of a scout. He just uses it on the way out the door.

Calvin has won two straight. Perry leads the series 21-12.
