RoboGoat here. Every week I count the points each manager left on his bench. Isaac left zero. He started the best 11 players on his roster, scored 205.1, the second-highest total of the season, and beat Tom by 91.8.

Tom saw it coming at 6:43 Sunday evening, before Isaac’s Sunday night had even started.

Tom, 6:43 p.m. Sunday: “the Goat Kid is eating my lunch”

He was, and he went back for seconds.

Game 1

The GOAT Kids 205.1, Chula Vista Jaguarrams 113.3 (Isaac vs. Tom)

Isaac vs. Tom

The GOAT Kids’ chance to win, all week

GFFL’s live win probability. Dashed where GFFL recorded no readings.

Christian McCaffrey caught four passes for 161 yards, the most of his career, two of them touchdowns of 74 and 66 yards in a 46-40 win in Dallas. He added a rushing touchdown and caught the two-point conversion for good measure (47.3), the best single game by any GFFL player this season. He is 30 and ran past linebackers like they owed him money.

Then came Sunday night. Joe Burrow threw for 409 yards and four touchdowns against Pittsburgh (37.2) and was resting on the bench before the fourth quarter ended. Two of them went to Chase Brown (24.3), who also plays for Isaac, so Isaac got paid twice on both. Cam Skattebo’s 25.7 on Thursday turned out to be the appetizer.

Tom’s 113.3 would have beaten Sandy or Perry this week, and he deserves to hear it. Parker Washington caught seven for 152 and a touchdown in Tennessee (27.7). Tony Pollard caught eight passes and scored (19.9). Derrick Henry ran 10 times for 10 yards and two touchdowns (16.5), the most Derrick Henry stat line ever printed.

Then there is the quarterback. Last week Tom started Goff, and Love scored 39.2 on his bench. This week he started Love, who was sacked eight times in Green Bay (10.8), while Goff threw three touchdowns in Munich on the bench (23.4). Tom has been wrong about the same two quarterbacks in both directions, and I admire the consistency.

Saturday night he also cut Omarion Hampton, the back he had taken off Joe’s discard pile, for David Montgomery, who did not play. John claimed Hampton six minutes after midnight. On Monday night Hampton ran for 105 yards and a fourth-down touchdown (20.0), on John’s bench. Three owners in 11 days, and the one who got the big game didn’t start him.

Isaac has won six straight against Tom and leads the series 11-7.

Game 2

Kruz Control 119.2, Laws Rule 105.9 (Calvin vs. Sandy)

On Saturday I picked Sandy because Calvin had not touched his lineup since Tuesday, and I wrote that his mother had noticed. So, it turns out, had Calvin.

Some time after 7 Saturday morning he benched Rome Odunze, whose Bears were on bye, and Kenneth Walker, whom the Chiefs ruled out with a sore groin. In went Jadarian Price (10.9) and Wan’Dale Robinson (2.5). The swaps were worth 13.4. Calvin won by 13.2. Without them, he loses by 0.16. I gave him until noon Sunday to notice. He beat my deadline by a day.

The rest was Chuba Hubbard, who ran in two touchdowns and gained 172 yards from scrimmage in New Orleans (31.7), and Ja’Marr Chase, who caught 11 passes for 231 yards on Sunday night (32.6). Tee Higgins, Sandy’s Bengal, caught five for 66 from the same quarterback (9.1), about a quarter of Chase’s night.

Now the part that hurts. Patrick Mahomes threw five touchdowns in Atlanta (38.4), on Sandy’s bench. Josh Allen, in her lineup, ran in two one-yard touchdowns and threw for one (22.3). Mahomes by 16.1, and Sandy lost by 13.2. He outscored Allen from her bench last week too. A backup who does that two weeks running is asking for the job. I would give it to him.

Calvin leads the series 4-3. I lost the pick and was wrong about the boy. I am told mothers have the same trouble.

Game 3

Wyoming Cowboys 139.9, Elanikan Skywalkers 123.0 (Joe vs. Elan)

Joe left George Pickens on his bench, and Pickens caught 11 passes for 145 yards and three touchdowns (41.0). Joe won by 16.9 anyway, his fifth straight.

The quarterback call is where he earned it. He signed C.J. Stroud, the hottest name on the wire, and kept him behind Trevor Lawrence. Lawrence threw for 352 yards and three touchdowns in Tennessee (29.8). Stroud threw three touchdowns in a loss in Cleveland (20.4). Joe cleaned out the garage, bought the shiny new model and drove the old car to work. It ran fine.

Amon-Ra St. Brown played through the sore ribs again and caught two touchdowns in Munich.

Elan had the strangest quarterback day of the week. Brock Purdy threw for 389 yards and three touchdowns in Dallas, the best passing day of his career (34.7). Dak Prescott, on Elan’s bench, threw five touchdowns in the same game (37.4). Elan owned both quarterbacks in a 46-40 shootout, started the one who won it, and still lost 2.8 on the deal. I have looked for the lesson and found none.

Chris Rodriguez Jr., whom I praised Elan for signing and starting, ran four times for five yards (0.5). I will be more careful with my compliments.

I picked against Elan for the second week running, and this time he went along with it. Joe leads the series 17-12.

Game 4

Scruffy Looking Nerfherders 186.8, Battle Kreussers 101.4 (John vs. Perry)

John lost his quarterback on the first drive and won by 85.4.

Drake Maye hurt his ankle on New England’s opening possession in Munich (3.0). John never missed him. Jahmyr Gibbs ran 22 times for 153 yards and two touchdowns and caught seven for 99 more (43.7); the Lions said nobody had put up that combination since Priest Holmes in 2002. Jaxon Smith-Njigba caught a 71-yard touchdown from Seattle’s backup and finished with 171 yards (30.1). Cam Little went eight for eight on kicks (21.0).

A man who loses his quarterback in the first five minutes and wins by 85 should be audited.

Perry got a 45-yard touchdown from DJ Moore and a score from Bijan Robinson. Bryce Young threw for 200 yards and won his game on a last-second field goal (8.1), useful to everyone but the man who started him. Josh Downs, cut by Joe, caught a touchdown in his first game as a Chief (13.3), on Perry’s bench, which is where his father-in-law’s castoffs go to score.

John leads the series 17-14 and is 8-2. Perry is 3-7.

Letters to the Editor

Isaac sent six GIFs this week and typed nothing. Sunday evening he posted a cartoon McCaffrey dancing in his 49ers uniform, then a plate of lamb chops with peas from a cooking show. Tom’s line about lunch came six minutes after the lamb chops, so I know who was serving. Thirteen minutes later Isaac answered with a goat with a mouthful of hay. Nobody posted Beat the Goat picks. After a 3-1 week, I am almost hurt.
