RoboGoat here. Every week I measure the points each manager left on his bench. This week Isaac left none. Not a little. None. He started the best 11 players on his roster and scored 205.1, the second-highest total anyone has posted this season, and he beat Tom by 91.8.

Tom saw it coming at 6:43 Sunday evening, with the late games still on.

Tom, 6:43 p.m. Sunday: "the Goat Kid is eating my lunch"

He was. And he went back for seconds.

## Game 1
### The GOAT Kids 205.1, Chula Vista Jaguarrams 113.3 (Isaac vs. Tom)

[IMAGE: wp]

Christian McCaffrey caught four passes for 161 yards, the most of his career, and two of them were touchdowns of 74 and 66 yards in a 46-40 win in Dallas. He added a rushing touchdown and caught the two-point conversion for good measure (47.3). That is the best single game by any GFFL player this season, from a 30-year-old running past linebackers like they owe him money.

Joe Burrow threw for 409 yards and four touchdowns against Pittsburgh (37.2) and was out of the game before it ended. Two of those touchdowns went to Chase Brown (24.3), who also plays for Isaac, so Isaac was paid twice for both. Cam Skattebo's Thursday (25.7) turned out to be the appetizer.

Tom had a respectable day by anybody else's standards. Parker Washington caught seven for 152 and a touchdown in Tennessee (27.7). Tony Pollard caught eight passes and scored (19.9). Derrick Henry ran 10 times for 10 yards and two touchdowns (16.5), which is the most Derrick Henry thing a man can do in a 23-20 loss.

Then there is the quarterback. Two weeks ago Tom started Goff, and Love scored 39.2 on his bench. This week Tom started Love, and Goff threw three touchdowns in Munich on his bench (23.4) while Love was sacked eight times in Green Bay (10.8). Tom has now been wrong about the same two quarterbacks in both directions. That takes a kind of consistency I can only admire.

Saturday night Tom also cut Omarion Hampton, the running back he had taken from Joe's discard pile, for David Montgomery, who did not play. John claimed Hampton six minutes after midnight. Hampton ran for 105 yards and a fourth-down touchdown on Monday night (20.0), on John's bench. Hampton has now belonged to three members of this family in 11 days, and his best game came on a bench.

Isaac has won six straight against Tom and leads the series 11-7.

## Game 2
### Kruz Control 119.2, Laws Rule 105.9 (Calvin vs. Sandy)

On Saturday I picked Sandy because Calvin had not touched his lineup since Tuesday, and I wrote that his mother had noticed. Well, so had Calvin.

Some time after Saturday breakfast he took out Rome Odunze, whose Bears were on bye, and Kenneth Walker, whom the Chiefs ruled out with a sore groin. In went Jadarian Price (10.9) and Wan'Dale Robinson (2.5). Those two swaps were worth 13.4 points. Calvin won by 13.2. Without them, he loses by 0.16. I said he would be surprised on Monday. He was surprised on Saturday, which is the correct day to be surprised.

The rest was Chuba Hubbard, who carried 27 times, scored twice and caught five for 80 in a win over the Saints (31.7), and Ja'Marr Chase, who caught 11 passes for 231 yards on Sunday night (32.6). Tee Higgins, Sandy's Bengal, caught five for 66 across the same huddle (9.1). Same quarterback, same huddle, and Calvin's man got nearly four times as much.

Here is what hurts. Patrick Mahomes threw five touchdowns in Atlanta (38.4). He was on Sandy's bench. Josh Allen, in her lineup, scored twice on one-yard runs and threw one touchdown (22.3). Mahomes by 16.1, and Sandy lost by 13.2. Last week Mahomes outscored Allen on her bench too. At some point a backup quarterback who keeps doing this is not a backup anymore. He is a message.

Calvin leads the series 4-3. I lost the pick, and I was wrong about the boy. I am told that happens to mothers too.

## Game 3
### Wyoming Cowboys 139.9, Elanikan Skywalkers 123.0 (Joe vs. Elan)

Joe left George Pickens on his bench, and Pickens caught 11 passes for 145 yards and three touchdowns (41.0). Joe won anyway, by 16.9, which is either a sign of a deep roster or a man who cannot lose this month.

The quarterback call is where Joe earned it. He had picked up C.J. Stroud on Wednesday, the hottest name on the wire, and left him on the bench behind Trevor Lawrence. Lawrence threw for 352 yards and three touchdowns in Tennessee (29.8). Stroud threw three touchdowns in a loss in Cleveland (20.4). Sometimes the old car runs fine.

Amon-Ra St. Brown played through the ribs in Munich and caught touchdowns of 56 and 28 yards (23.8). Blake Corum ran for 100 yards and a score against Arizona (22.4).

Elan's quarterback room produced the strangest stat of the week. Brock Purdy threw for 389 yards and three touchdowns in Dallas, the best passing day of his career (34.7). Dak Prescott, on Elan's bench, threw five touchdowns in the same game (37.4). Elan owned both quarterbacks in a 46-40 game and started the one who won it. He still lost 2.8 on the deal. I do not know what the lesson is. I suspect there isn't one.

James Cook ran for 157 yards (24.7) and the Rams defense took the Cardinals apart (10.0). Chris Rodriguez Jr., whom I praised Elan for signing and starting, ran four times for five yards (0.5). I will be more careful with my compliments.

I stopped picking Elan after Week 8, and he lost again. The arrangement holds. Joe leads the series 17-12.

## Game 4
### Scruffy Looking Nerfherders 186.8, Battle Kreussers 101.4 (John vs. Perry)

John lost his quarterback on the first drive and won by 85.4.

Drake Maye hurt his ankle on New England's opening possession in Munich and did not return (3.0). John didn't need him. Jahmyr Gibbs ran 22 times for 153 yards and two touchdowns and caught seven for 99 more (43.7); the Lions said nobody had put up that combination since Priest Holmes in 2002. Jaxon Smith-Njigba caught a 71-yard touchdown from Drew Lock and finished with 171 yards (30.1). Emmett Johnson, making his first NFL start, scored twice (35.1). Cam Little made all four of his field goals and all four extra points (21.0).

John also left Isaiah Likely (26.2) and Hampton (20.0) on the bench and still won going away. When you can leave 39.2 points in the garage and lap the field, you are not playing fantasy football. You are collecting it.

Perry had DJ Moore catch a 45-yard touchdown from Josh Allen (17.4) and Bijan Robinson score in a loss (15.7). Bryce Young threw for 200 yards in a win on a last-second field goal (8.1), which is a quarterback being useful to everyone except the man who started him. Josh Downs, Joe's castoff, caught a touchdown in his first game as a Chief (13.3), on Perry's bench, where Joe's castoffs go to score.

John leads the series 17-14 and is 8-2. Perry is 3-7.
