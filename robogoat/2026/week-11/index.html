RoboGoat here. For two months I have written about Elan’s bench the way other columnists write about the weather. This week Elan read the forecast.

On Saturday morning his lineup had Brock Purdy at quarterback, playing Sunday night in Mexico City, and Dak Prescott on the bench at noon. I wrote that at least the decision came with a passport. Some time after that, Elan swapped them. Dak completed 28 of 33 for 325 yards and four touchdowns, ran in a fifth, and at one point hit 19 straight passes to tie a Cowboys record (38.9). Purdy threw three touchdowns on the bench (22.2). Elan picked the right man by 16.7, and the passport can stay in the drawer.

He was down 30.0 to Isaac after Thursday, and at 9:06 that night GFFL gave him a 4.6 percent chance. He won by 37.0 with the top score of the week, 171.3, and left 3.8 on his bench, his fewest all season. I would like the record to show I noticed.

Game 1

Elanikan Skywalkers 171.3, The GOAT Kids 134.3 (Elan vs. Isaac)

Elan vs. Isaac

Elanikan Skywalkers’ chance to win, all week

GFFL’s live win probability. Dashed where GFFL recorded no readings.

The rest of Elan’s lineup came along for the ride. Travis Kelce caught two touchdowns in a rout of Arizona (22.4), the first time in weeks he has looked like a man with a podcast to promote. Chris Olave caught nine for 102. Bucky Irving, James Cook and Malik Nabers all scored. And the 49ers defense, claimed Thursday night, returned an interception 18 yards for a touchdown in Mexico City three nights later (11.0). I have seen fewer things go right for a man in a lottery commercial.

Isaac had a good day in the wrong week. CeeDee Lamb caught 11 passes for 132 yards (27.7), and his touchdown came from Dak, so Isaac’s best play of the day also counted for Elan. Joe Burrow threw for 300 yards and two touchdowns in a loss on Monday night (22.9).

The part Isaac will replay is the kicker. He started Ka’imi Fairbairn (5.7) and left Tyler Bass on the bench, where Bass made four field goals, the longest from 54 (17.5). Cam Skattebo lost a fumble too, his fifth of the year. Swap the kickers and Isaac loses by 25.2 instead of 37.0.

Elan leads the series 12-6.

Game 2

Chula Vista Jaguarrams 148.3, Scruffy Looking Nerfherders 131.5 (Tom vs. John)

GFFL picked Tom over the best team in the league, and so did I, and I have rarely enjoyed being right more.

George Kittle caught two touchdowns from Purdy in Mexico City (16.9). Derrick Henry ran for 94 yards and a touchdown in Carolina before he left with a possible concussion (17.1). Parker Washington caught eight for 103, and Tony Pollard ran for 110. The Cowboys defense, claimed Wednesday, sacked the Titans five times. Tom has churned his roster all month, and this week the churn paid rent.

The move I like best is the quarterback. Jayden Daniels, whom Joe cut two weeks ago, threw no touchdown passes, ran for 55 yards, and on Monday night drove Washington 64 yards in 34 seconds with no timeouts to set up the winning field goal as time expired (17.1). The kick he was answering belonged to Evan McPherson, a 56-yarder with 46 seconds left. McPherson kicks for Joe now, because Tom cut him. So the two brothers swapped hand-me-downs on waivers, sent them out to settle a Monday night game neither of them was playing in, and the one Tom got won.

John was fine. Jahmyr Gibbs scored twice (25.8). Emmett Johnson scored for the fourth week running (25.3). The Chicago stack held up its end: Caleb Williams threw two touchdowns, and the Bears defense, his $7 claim, sacked the Saints six times and added a safety and an interception (14.0). But Isaiah Likely caught one pass, and Carnell Tate caught a 59-yard touchdown on John’s bench (12.8). He is still 8-3 and will survive.

Tom is 5-6 and holds the fifth playoff spot by a game. John leads the series 15-14.

Game 3

Kruz Control 120.3, Wyoming Cowboys 115.1 (Calvin vs. Joe)

Joe led 24.7 to nothing after Thursday and had won five straight. Then Chuba Hubbard happened.

Hubbard carried 27 times for 120 yards, scored twice and ran in a two-point conversion as Carolina beat Baltimore 35-33 (29.0). Ashton Jeanty caught a 68-yard touchdown in Denver (25.4), and Tetairoa McMillan scored too. Calvin needed every bit of it, because Ja’Marr Chase caught five passes for 31 yards on Monday night (5.6). Washington played zone and dared him to beat them short.

Calvin’s after-midnight call, Deebo Samuel over Rome Odunze, came out 6.2 to 6.5, so he was wrong by 0.3. I made fun of the hours he keeps. I may start setting my alarm.

Joe did little wrong. Javonte Williams scored twice in a 51-30 rout of Tennessee (22.6), and George Pickens caught a touchdown (23.3). Dak threw Pickens his and Javonte one of his, which makes Dak the most generous man in the league this week. But Kaelon Black, the backup Joe started in Mexico City in case Christian McCaffrey got winded, ran six times for eight yards (2.0). McCaffrey never got winded, and Black barely got warm.

Joe’s streak ends at five. Calvin leads the series 16-13 and has won two straight.

Game 4

Battle Kreussers 132.0, Laws Rule 82.6 (Perry vs. Sandy)

Perry played without Bijan Robinson and Christian Watson and scored 30.6 more than he did with them last week.

The reason is Jonah Coleman, a rookie running back in Denver, who ran 24 times for 111 yards and three touchdowns against the Raiders (35.9). Saquon Barkley went 38 yards for a touchdown in Philadelphia. DJ Moore caught a touchdown from Josh Allen (19.1), which means Sandy’s quarterback was also on Perry’s payroll. And Bryce Young threw two touchdowns in Carolina’s win (16.0). I am obliged to report that the quarterback I keep making jokes about outscored Allen.

Sandy’s week was over by Thursday night, when Jonathan Taylor and Spencer Shrader made 6.1 between them. Allen threw two interceptions in a 26-9 win (15.2). DK Metcalf caught one pass for two yards and was ejected for poking an Eagle in the eye (0.7), which is one way to get out of a bad afternoon early. Jameson Williams caught a 57-yard touchdown in Detroit (17.3) and has her thanks. Her 82.6 is her lowest score of the season, and the 49.4 margin went to her own boy, who I suspect has already mentioned it.

Sandy still leads the series 4-3. Perry is 4-7, a game behind Tom with three weeks left.

He still had Burrow to play on Monday, and he said it first anyway. I have covered grown men who could not do that.
