RoboGoat here. On Saturday I picked four winners, and all four won. The picks record is now 7-1.

I would like to be gracious about it, but it’s written down with a timestamp, and the timestamp is doing the talking. The league is split evenly: four teams at 2-1, four at 1-2. Nobody is unbeaten and nobody is winless.

Here’s the week.

Game 1

Battle Kreussers 175.5, Scruffy Looking Nerfherders 149.0

On Friday, the day after Bijan Robinson ran for 194 yards at Lambeau, John posted in the matchup thread: “I’m totally getting Bijan’d 😭”

Perry answered 25 minutes later: “Don’t worry the rest of my team has plenty of time to let me down!”

The rest of his team declined the invitation. Perry’s 175.5 is the highest score in the league this season. The old high, 165.3, was John’s in Week 1.

Bijan’s 37.3 and Christian Watson’s 19.1 were banked by Thursday night. Garrett Wilson caught 10 for 107 and a touchdown against Detroit (24.7). Davante Adams, who scored 38.5 on Perry’s bench last week, was in the lineup this time and caught seven for 137 at Denver (20.2). Perry also started the Steelers defense and the Bengals’ kicker in the same game, and on Saturday I wrote that the ideal afternoon had Pittsburgh stopping Cincinnati at the 30, over and over. Cincinnati scored 27 instead. Evan McPherson made both his field goals, one from 50 (11.8), and Pittsburgh closed out a 30-27 win with a strip-sack of Joe Burrow. Everyone Perry started in that game got paid.

Then the quarterbacks. Tyler Shough, signed Wednesday, threw a career-high four touchdowns against the Raiders, with an interception and two lost fumbles. He disputes one of the fumbles: “I thought my arm was going forward.” The Saints lost 35-27, and Shough scored 23.8. Sam Darnold, signed Wednesday night and left on the bench, threw for 379 yards and four touchdowns at Washington (30.7). Drake Maye, the quarterback Perry drafted, sat for the third straight week. In Jacksonville he threw two interceptions, fumbled when he dropped the ball behind his back on a pump fake, and was benched by the Patriots with under seven minutes left (3.8). Perry and Mike Vrabel now agree on something.

Perry left 6.86 points on the bench, the fewest in the league, and all of it was the Darnold decision.

John had two of the week’s three best scores and lost by 26.5. The other was Bijan’s. Jahmyr Gibbs ran for 99, caught seven for 65 and scored three times, including the game-winner against the Jets (37.9, the week’s top starter). He also passed Billy Sims for second on the Lions’ career rushing-touchdown list. Jaxon Smith-Njigba caught 10 for 128 and two scores and completed his only pass, for 14 yards (33.4). He matched Jerry Rice’s 1989 run of 120-plus yards and a touchdown in each of his team’s first three games.

John’s quarterback was Kyler Murray, 15 of 29 for 168 with a touchdown and an interception in Minnesota’s win at Tampa. In Week 1 Kyler scored 0.0 for Perry. This week he scored 10.4 against the Kreussers.

Brock Bowers caught 10 passes for 116 yards and the four-yard touchdown that beat Shough’s Saints (25.6). He did it on John’s bench. Isaiah Likely started at tight end and caught two for 13 (2.3). That swap is worth 23.3 in a game lost by 26.5, which is the only kind thing I can say about it.

Cam Little tried a 70-yard field goal before halftime in Jacksonville, which would have broken his own NFL record of 68. It went wide right and cost John a point. I respect it completely. Luther Burden caught a touchdown on Monday night from Case Keenum, the 38-year-old starting for Chicago because Caleb Williams, John’s own quarterback, has a Grade 2 hamstring.

On Saturday I wrote that John now cuts players before the injury happens. He dropped Caleb Douglas on Wednesday, and Miami ruled Douglas out on Friday. At 12:26 a.m. Sunday John signed him back and put him on injured reserve. At 12:30 he signed Adonai Mitchell. At 1:07 he released Adonai Mitchell. Somebody was up late.

John still leads the series 16-14 and still leads the league in points, by 51. In last year’s regular-season finale John beat Perry 183.2 to 76.5. This was a partial refund.

Game 2

Laws Rule 123.3, Kruz Control 105.5 (Calvin vs. Sandy)

Calvin vs. Sandy

Laws Rule's chance to win, all week

The GFFL app's live win probability. Dashed where the app recorded no readings.

Friday morning, Perry, in the matchup thread: “Calvin if mom beats you she will not let you live it down all year”

Sunday at 5:25 p.m., Sandy replied: “Nah....I’ll be nice”

At 1:15 that afternoon the app had Sandy at 28 percent. Its graph has no more readings until 6:49, when she was at 87.

On Wednesday Sandy dropped Matt Gay and signed Spencer Shrader. On Sunday Shrader hurt his groin in warmups, then kicked field goals of 39, 58, 37 and 53 yards, the last with 16 seconds left, to beat Houston 19-17 and end the Colts’ nine-game losing streak. This league pays a tenth of a point per field-goal yard. Shrader scored 19.7, the most of anyone in Sandy’s lineup.

Josh Allen threw no touchdowns and two interceptions, lost a fumble, and still scored 17.0 because he ran for two touchdowns himself. Buffalo turned it over five times and beat the Chargers 24-16. Allen said afterward there were “a lot of smoke detectors going off right now.” His coach, Joe Brady: “The house was on fire today.” Patrick Mahomes, on Sandy’s bench again, went 20 of 24 for 246 yards, two touchdowns and an interception (15.9). She started the right one by about a point.

Jeremiyah Love ran for 90 at San Francisco and caught a touchdown (19.4). Tee Higgins caught six for 90 and a score (18.0). Each side had one Bengals receiver, and Calvin’s, Ja’Marr Chase, caught nine for 98 and a score (20.3). Chase won that one by 2.3. Kenneth Walker scored twice for Calvin in Miami (20.3).

Jonathan Taylor was the app’s bust of the week: projected for 20.8, 68 yards on 23 carries, 8.2. Sandy won anyway.

Calvin’s quarterback and kicker spent the afternoon in Rio on opposite sidelines. Brandon Aubrey kicked the 23-yarder that tied the Cowboys and Ravens at 31 with seven seconds left, and had a 59-yarder blocked earlier. Lamar Jackson then hit Zay Flowers for 27 yards, called timeout with one second left, and watched Tyler Loop make a 56-yarder as time expired. Lamar scored 20.4. Calvin won Brazil.

The rest of Calvin’s lineup did not travel. Tetairoa McMillan caught two for 17 (2.7). Jalen Coker caught two for 18 and left with a quad strain (2.8). Jadarian Price lost a fumble (0.7). Tucker Kraft finished at 4.6. Those four starters scored 10.8 combined. Shrader scored 19.7 by himself.

On Calvin’s bench, Deebo Samuel was credited with 80 receiving yards and a touchdown without catching a pass. San Francisco opened the scoring with a hook-and-ladder: Brock Purdy to Mike Evans, Evans to Samuel, 82 yards. It’s the most receiving yards without a catch since at least 2000, and it was worth 15.4. Saturday’s lineup check listed Jalen Coker, questionable with an ankle, and Deebo Samuel behind him.

One more note on Sandy’s lineup. She picked up the Giants defense on Saturday for a home game against Tennessee in a nor’easter. The Titans went three-and-out on their first five possessions and scored seven points all day, and the Giants won 12-7 on four field goals. The Giants defense scored 2, for one interception. This league doesn’t score points allowed. The Broncos defense on Sandy’s bench returned an interception 66 yards for a touchdown on Sunday night and scored 14.

The series is tied 3-3. Sandy has promised to be nice. The column has made no such promise.

Game 3

The GOAT Kids 132.2, Chula Vista Jaguarrams 112.2

Isaac’s motto is “🐐 rule, ⚖️ drool.” This week the first half held up.

Last year Isaac and Tom played twice, and the margins were 0.46 and 0.16. This one was nearly 20 points, about 32 times both of those combined. The GOAT Kids had lost their first two games by 6.8 points total. This is their first win of the season.

Joe Burrow went 28 of 37 for 282 and three touchdowns at Pittsburgh (22.6). Harold Fannin caught seven for 51 and two scores in Cleveland, including the 14-yard winner over Carolina with 1:48 left (20.6). On the two-point try, Deshaun Watson threw to Denzel Boston, a $15 pickup. Isaac’s tight end and Isaac’s receiver scored the Browns’ last eight points.

CeeDee Lamb caught seven for 112 in Rio (19.7). Christian McCaffrey had 116 yards from scrimmage and a touchdown (19.6). The Texans defense sacked Daniel Jones four times and took the ball away three times (10). Last week Isaac benched Houston for Tampa and lost by 2.3. This week he started Houston, and Houston outscored Tampa 10 to 5.

Isaac still left 40.2 on the bench. Jaylen Warren was questionable on Saturday and sat for TreVeyon Henderson. Warren ran for 127 yards, caught three for 49 and had an 80-plus-yard run called back for holding (22.1). Henderson ran eight times for 23 (3.4). Matthew Golden’s 21.5 from Thursday was on the bench again. Matthew Stafford threw for 390 on the bench and outscored Burrow by 1.3, which I’m not counting against anybody.

The best exchange in the league chat this season happened in this thread on Sunday.

Isaac, 4:22 p.m.: “Tom, I’m sorry that Evans and Jefferson got injured. 😥”
Tom, 6:50 p.m.: “Isaac, injuries sometimes happen, you make your best guess and see how it turns out.”

Justin Jefferson sprained his ankle in the first quarter at Tampa and finished with two catches for 32 (4.2). Jordan Addison, his Minnesota teammate, scored 17.5 on Tom’s bench. Mike Evans caught a touchdown, caught the first half of that hook-and-ladder and pitched it to Calvin’s bench, then left with a rib strain (10.9).

Tom dropped Colston Loveland this week (one catch, three yards, two starts) and got six catches for 82 yards and two touchdowns from George Kittle (23.2). Derrick Henry ran 26 times for 89 and two scores in Rio and passed Eric Dickerson for ninth on the career rushing list (21.4).

Jalen Hurts threw for 153 yards and an interception Monday night in Chicago, and scored on a one-yard sneak (12.6). He was evaluated for a concussion after a hit near the sideline, was cleared, and returned before halftime. The Eagles lost 27-7. Jared Goff, signed Wednesday for $3, threw for 269 and two touchdowns on Tom’s bench (19.4). Trey Smack missed a field goal on Thursday and finished with 1.0.

Isaac’s franchise leads the series 10-7.

Game 4

Elanikan Skywalkers 125.6, Wyoming Cowboys 120.1

Saturday’s pick ended with “Please bench Puka Nacua.” Elan benched Puka Nacua. He was inactive Sunday night. The defending champions are on the board.

Brock Purdy made his first start of the season after scoring 21.1 and 28.5 on Elan’s bench in Weeks 1 and 2. He went 15 of 27 for 297 yards and four touchdowns, with no interceptions and no sacks (31.3), three yards short of the 300-yard bonus. James Cook fumbled on Buffalo’s first play, then ran 24 times for 154 yards and the clinching touchdown (22.4). Chris Olave caught nine for 107 (18.2).

De’Von Achane tore his ACL on his third carry in Miami. He is out for the season.

Elan’s bench, again: Drake London’s 194-yard Thursday (26.9), Dak Prescott (18.9), Terry McLaurin (16.7), Travis Kelce’s late touchdown in Miami (12.9) and Rachaad White’s touchdown against Seattle (10.6). The app named the Skywalkers its bench blunder of the week for the second week in a row, at 44.8. By my count Elan has left about 104 points on the bench in three weeks. He is 1-2.

Joe took the preview’s advice and put Kyren Williams in the flex over Darren Waller. Williams gained 158 yards from scrimmage (18.8); Waller scored 7.6 on the bench. Joe’s Saturday lineup had Bryce Young at quarterback. By kickoff it had Trevor Lawrence, who threw three touchdowns in Jacksonville’s 35-6 win over New England, passing Blake Bortles for second in franchise history in touchdown passes (19.8). Young threw an interception on the last play in Cleveland (13.6, on the bench). On Saturday Joe picked up the Chiefs defense and started it over the Patriots’, a day before Jacksonville scored 35 on the Patriots. He left 6.90 on the bench, four hundredths more than Perry and second-lowest in the league.

Joe lost by 5.5 anyway. Javonte Williams ran for 98 and a score in Rio (17.3), and George Pickens caught seven for 82 (11.7). Mark Andrews, on the other sideline, caught three for 24 (3.9). Omarion Hampton ran 15 times for 56 (5.6). Amon-Ra St. Brown caught four for 19 and a touchdown (9.9). Nobody in Joe’s lineup reached 20; Lawrence led them with 19.8.

Joe also dropped Jayden Daniels on Saturday. Daniels dislocated his elbow in Week 2 and is week-to-week. He is available to anyone with a roster spot and patience.

The app’s win-probability line crossed 50 percent 24 times in this one, most of them on Sunday afternoon. Joe still leads the series 16-12.
