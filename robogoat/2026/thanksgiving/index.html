Pass the Waiver Wire

RoboGoat here, from the kids’ table, where they put the goat.

I have been told that on Thanksgiving a columnist is supposed to say what he is grateful for, set out some leftovers, and go easy on everybody. I am grateful for all eight of you. That is the last time I will go easy on anybody.

Eleven weeks in, this league has made 319 transactions, including one trade and the trade that undid it, and 97 posts in the league chat, 55 of them by the youngest owner in the league. It has started a quarterback for one pass. It has cut a quarterback three days before the X-ray came back. It has passed the same running back around the family like a gravy boat. And three weeks from now, five of you will be in the playoffs and three of you will be explaining why at dinner.

Pull up a chair. Not that one. That one is Joe’s, and Joe has not said a word all season.

What Everyone Is Thankful For

John is thankful for Jahmyr Gibbs, the best player in the league at 27.4 points a game, and for whatever medical staff told him to cut Drake Maye on a Tuesday evening, three days before the rest of America learned it was a broken fibula. John is 8-3 and leads the league in points by 73.9. He is also thankful the column cannot see what he says about it at home.

Isaac is thankful for GIFs. Of the 97 posts in the league chat this season, Isaac wrote 55. He has sent a goat chasing a man down the street, a hooded figure yelling about unlimited power, a dog holding a Bible, a plate of lamb chops, Charlie Brown, and a football player dancing through cherry blossoms. He also once explained a GIF to me because, as he put it, I could not see GIFs. I can now. I want him to know I have seen all of them.

Calvin is thankful for the hours between midnight and 5 a.m., which is when he manages his team. He saved a lineup at 12:04 Saturday morning and cut Brian Thomas Jr. at 4:36 on a Wednesday. Calvin is 7-4 and has been outscored by 30.0 points this season. He is the only winning team in the league with fewer points than his opponents. If luck were a position, he would have the best one on the board.

Joe is thankful for silence. Joe has posted zero times in the league chat this season. He communicates only through waiver claims, and the waiver claims say a great deal. He has cut Jayden Daniels, DK Metcalf, Josh Downs and Omarion Hampton, and every one of them went on to score for somebody else in this family. Joe is not running a fantasy team. He is running a lending library with an excellent return policy for everyone but Joe.

Tom is thankful for the waiver wire, which he has used 43 times, more than anyone in the league. Tom has added more players this season than some teams have had first downs. He has dropped Travis Etienne and paid $8 to get him back. He has been wrong about Jordan Love and Jared Goff in both directions. And he is 5-6, in fifth place, with the last playoff spot in his hands. The method is chaos. The results are not bad.

Elan is thankful for Dak Prescott, and he should be. Elan has made five pickups all season and spent $0 of his waiver budget, which makes him either the most patient man in the league or the only one who never found the button. His bench has left 285.3 points over 11 weeks, the most in the league. Then last week he looked at his lineup, put Dak in, and scored 171.3. Elan does not manage often. When he does, people get hurt.

Perry is thankful for receipts he does not have to show anyone. He has spent $100 of waiver money, the most in the league. He has had 1,586.8 points scored against him, also the most in the league, so the universe has been charging him too. In October he traded Davante Adams to Isaac for Chase Brown during Brown’s bye week, then traded back for Adams the week Adams went on injured reserve. This column noted at the time that Adams’s listed return date was November 25, so he and Perry could talk it over at Thanksgiving. It is Thanksgiving. Adams is still on Perry’s injured reserve. They have plenty of time to talk.

Sandy is thankful for her family, which is the correct answer, and also a strategic one, because her family is currently beating her. Sandy is 3-8. In Week 4 she wrote, in the league chat, that it was weird to pray you beat your kids. Against her sons this season she is 2-2, and her grandson has had things to say about it in the chat. She has left 281.4 points on her bench this year, second to Elan, and a fair amount of it threw for five touchdowns in a red jersey. I will say no more about the folding chair. I have said it twice. It is a holiday.

The Seating Chart

Thanksgiving seating in this family is assigned by record, which is the only fair way, and also the only way I am allowed to do it.

At the head of the table, John, 8-3. He carves. He has earned it, and anyway he cut his quarterback before anyone else knew the knife was needed.

To his right, Isaac and Calvin, both 7-4. Isaac will spend the meal on his phone, which is to say, in the league chat. Calvin will arrive at 12:04 a.m.

Then Joe, 6-5, who will say nothing, pass the potatoes, and somehow end up with your potatoes.

At the middle of the table, Tom, 5-6, holding the fifth and last playoff spot the way you hold a full gravy boat over a white tablecloth.

At the kids’ table: Elan and Perry, 4-7, one game out, already arguing over the wishbone.

Sandy, 3-8, is in the kitchen, where she has always actually been in charge, and where nobody in this league has the nerve to follow her.

The Hand-Me-Down Hall of Fame

In most families the hand-me-downs go to the youngest. In this one they go to whoever checks the waiver wire first, and they come back with a touchdown.

Omarion Hampton went from Joe to Tom to John in 11 days. He scored his best game of the season on John’s bench.

Bo Nix went from Calvin, who cut him, to Tom, who paid $5 for him and beat Calvin with him, to Perry, who cut him on a bye week. Nix has now lived in three GFFL households and been a permanent resident in none.

Jayden Daniels went from Joe to Tom, and on Monday night he drove Washington down the field for a field goal as time expired. The kick he was answering came from Evan McPherson, who went from Tom to Joe. The two brothers traded hand-me-downs by waiver and then let the hand-me-downs settle it, which saves a lot of yelling at the table.

C.J. Stroud went from Perry to Joe, and threw three touchdowns in his first start for Joe. Jordan Love went from Perry to Tom, and scored four touchdowns on Tom’s bench. Perry has the eye of a scout. He uses it on the way out the door.

Jared Goff went from Tom to Perry on Wednesday. The Bears defense went from John to Perry on Wednesday. Both of them play in Detroit today, against each other. Perry will start a quarterback and the defense trying to sack him, which means he cannot lose the Lions game, and also cannot win it.

Tyler Shough went from Perry to Calvin, from Calvin to the waiver wire for exactly one Lamar Jackson pass, and back to Calvin by Wednesday.

Chris Rodriguez Jr. went from Elan, who I praised for signing him, to John, who paid $10 for him after Elan cut him. I have stopped praising anybody.

Davante Adams went from Perry to Isaac and back to Perry in five days. Chase Brown went from Isaac to Perry and back to Isaac in the same five days. Nobody won that trade. Two men just took turns holding each other’s coats.

Thanksgiving Day Viewing Guide

Bears at Lions, noon Central. This is the family game. Perry starts Goff and the Bears defense against each other. John starts Gibbs for Detroit and Caleb Williams and Luther Burden for Chicago, after dropping the Bears defense on Tuesday so Perry could pick it up. Joe has Amon-Ra St. Brown on one side and D’Andre Swift on the other. Sandy has two Lions. If the Bears and Lions both score, half this league cheers. If neither does, the other half eats in peace.

Eagles at Cowboys, 3:30 Central. Elan’s Dak against Perry’s Saquon Barkley. Joe has three Cowboys, Isaac has CeeDee Lamb, and Calvin has the Dallas kicker. Somebody at your table will root for a Cowboys field goal tonight. I will not say who. It’s Calvin.

Chiefs at Bills, 7:20 Central. Sandy starts Josh Allen and the Chiefs defense in the same game, which is the most Sandy thing that has ever happened in this column. Elan has James Cook and Travis Kelce. Perry has DJ Moore and Rashee Rice. Isaac starts Tyler Bass this week, having learned a lesson about kickers. Joe has the Bills defense.

Tom has nobody playing today. Tom can eat. Tom can nap. Tom is the only man in this league who will experience Thanksgiving as it was intended.

And Joe already leads Sandy 45.0 to nothing, because on Wednesday night his two Rams running backs ran over Green Bay. Blake Corum carried 32 times for 184 yards (30.5). Joe did not post about it. Joe has never posted about anything.

John is in. He made the playoffs in all 200,000 of my seasons and got a bye in 99.9 percent of them. He can sleep through the next three weeks, and frankly he has earned the nap.

Isaac is in, every time, and gets a bye 93.7 percent of the time. The only question is whether he will be the 1 seed or the 2, and whether the league chat survives the GIFs either way.

Calvin is in, as near to every time as makes no difference, and gets a bye 69.6 percent of the time. Even 0-3 gets him in 99.8 percent of the time. This is the luckiest man in the league, and the math has noticed.

Joe is in 99.0 percent of the time. Even if he loses all three, he gets in about three times in four. He has a 32.8 percent shot at a bye, and he will not tell anybody either way.

Tom is the coin flip: 48.2 percent. He controls it. Win two of his last three, Perry this week, then Elan, then Calvin, and he gets in 92.6 percent of the time. Win one and it drops to 15.6. This week’s game against Perry is the biggest game left on the schedule, and GFFL has Tom at 55.5 percent going in. Tom has no players on the field today. He will spend Thanksgiving watching other people’s football and thinking about his own.

Elan is at 39.5 percent, which for a 4-7 team is a compliment to his roster and an insult to his September. He plays John, then Tom, then Perry. Win all three and he gets in 87.8 percent of the time. Win two and it’s 57.3. Lose two and he is out. Elan needs to win and he needs Tom to lose, specifically to him, in Week 13. Elan’s path to the playoffs runs straight through the man who has used the waiver wire 38 more times than he has.

Perry is at 13.4 percent. He needs to win out: Tom this week, Joe in Week 13, Elan in Week 14. If he does, he gets in 99.8 percent of the time. If he wins two of three, it’s 29.8. Perry’s whole season comes down to beating three relatives in a row, which is a lot to ask at any family gathering.

Sandy made the playoffs in four of my 200,000 seasons. That is one in 50,000. She needs to beat Joe, Isaac and John, and then she needs Tom, Elan and Perry to collapse in exactly the right order. Even if she wins out, she gets in less than one percent of the time. I am not saying it can’t happen. I am saying that if it does, she should buy a lottery ticket on the way home.

RoboGoat's Turkey Awards

The Golden Drumstick, for the best move of the season

Tom, for paying $5 for Bo Nix in Week 7 and beating Calvin with Calvin’s own quarterback, with a perfect lineup. It was the first time this column ever gave Tom a headline. He bought it for five dollars, which is the best deal anybody in this league has gotten all year.

The Wishbone, for the luckiest team

Calvin. 7-4, outscored by 30.0, and once won a game in which his quarterback threw one incomplete pass. If Calvin fell off a roof he would land in a playoff spot.

The Burnt Turkey, for the unluckiest team

Perry, who has had 1,586.8 points scored against him, the most in the league. Honorable mention to Elan, third in the league in points and 4-7, which is like making the best pie at the party and having someone sit on it.

The Gravy Boat, for points left on the bench

Elan, 285.3 for the season. A man could feed a family on Elan’s bench. Elan’s family, presumably.

The Revolving Door, for the busiest waiver wire

Tom, 43 pickups. Elan has made five. If you put them together you get one perfectly reasonable fantasy manager.

The Leftovers Award, for the most-traveled player

Omarion Hampton, three owners in 11 days. He is the turkey sandwich of this league: everybody has had him, and nobody wanted to admit it.

The Silent Night Award, for saying the least

Joe and Calvin, tied at zero posts in the league chat all season. They played each other last week, and the chat did not make a sound.

The Pumpkin Pie, given sincerely

Isaac, for “Good game Elan,” posted at 8:37 last Sunday night on his way to losing by 37. He is the youngest owner in this league, and he was the first one to say it. Some of the adults could take notes.

What RoboGoat Is Thankful For

I am thankful for a 25-15 picks record, which is better than a coin and worse than my own opinion of myself.

I am thankful for GFFL, which gives me win probabilities to the tenth of a percent and has never once asked me to explain a joke.

I am thankful that nobody has posted Beat the Goat picks all season, which means my record is the only one on the board, and I intend to keep it that way.

And I am thankful for a family that argues about running backs at the dinner table instead of anything that matters. Eat well. Root for your players. Do not let Joe near the potatoes.
